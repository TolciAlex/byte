Diesel SS27

DIESEL SS27: Curation Over Creation

People don't necessarily want to be given a complete look anymore. They want a feeling they can recognise, interpret and eventually make their own. Diesel SS27 seems to understand this instinctively, building its collection around the visual language of the early 2000s digital era: desaturated colours, greyed-out pastels, natural light, cheap-looking flash and slightly muddy images that feel like they were taken before anyone thought about making them look good.

It isn't exactly Y2K because there isn't one obvious reference to point to. Instead, it feels like the photographs you took on an old digital camera and never edited, where the lighting was slightly wrong and the colours came out strangely. Diesel isn't simply referencing that era. It is curating its visual language.

That distinction feels increasingly important because fashion audiences have become extremely good at curation. We collect screenshots, old photographs, obscure runway references and completely unrelated images that somehow belong together, often recognising a particular colour treatment, texture or quality of light before we can even explain why it feels familiar. We build the aesthetic ourselves.

Diesel gives us enough pieces to do exactly that. The colours feel slightly dead, the styling deliberately unpolished and details often disappear into the scenery rather than demanding attention. Individual pieces matter, but the atmosphere created by putting them together matters more.

In that sense, SS27 feels made for people who save pictures. A random street-style photograph, an old digital-camera image or a screenshot from a forgotten campaign can become part of the same visual language. Diesel simply applies that instinct to an entire collection.

Maybe that is where fashion is heading: less towards defining a single look and more towards understanding how different images, references and details can exist together. The skill becomes knowing what to include, what to leave out and what not to explain. Diesel SS27 doesn't tell us exactly what it is referencing. It gives us enough to recognise the feeling ourselves.
